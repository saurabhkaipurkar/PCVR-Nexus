How PCVR Nexus works

To understand what data is involved, it helps to know what happens when you use the apps:

  1. Finding your PC. PCVR Nexus Host announces itself on your local network every few seconds with a short message containing your PC’s name (its Windows computer name) and the network ports it listens on. The phone listens for this message to show your PC in the list.
  2. Pairing once. When you tap your PC, the phone sends its model name (for example “samsung SM‑S918B”) and a random ID the app created when it was first opened. The PC shows a 4‑digit code, which you type on the phone. If it matches, the PC gives the phone a pairing key so it can reconnect later without the code.
  3. Streaming. The PC captures its screen and the sound playing on it, compresses the picture into video, and sends both to the phone. The phone shows them, flat or side by side for a VR headset. Nothing is recorded.
  4. Looking around. The phone reads its motion sensors and sends its orientation to the PC many times a second. The PC turns this into mouse movement or a virtual game controller, so the game camera follows your head.

What PCVR Nexus stores on your phone

All of this lives in the app’s private storage. Other apps can’t read it, and it is not included in Android cloud backups.

  • App ID: a random identifier created on first launch.
  • Paired PCs: each PC’s name, its pairing key, and when you last used it.
  • Last connection: the address and ports of the PC you last connected to, so it can reconnect quickly.
  • Settings: stream quality, codec, theme, connection type, VR headset fit and similar choices.
  • Stream logs: in a small database (see section 6), plus up to three log files you exported for sharing.

What PCVR Nexus Host stores on your PC

Files are kept in %LOCALAPPDATA%\GameStreamServer on your PC:

  • Paired phones (trusted-devices.json): each phone’s model name, its app ID, a hash of its pairing key, and when it was paired and last used. Up to 32 phones; the oldest are removed first.
  • Settings (app-settings.json): theme, tray and notification choices.
  • Capture method (last-capture-pipeline.txt): which screen-capture method worked last time, so the next start is faster.
  • Crash report (crash.log): only if the app crashes, the time and the technical error. It stays on your PC.

The host also writes two Windows settings: a “start with Windows” entry, only if you switch that on, and a graphics preference for its video encoder so screen capture uses the right GPU.

Stream logs

When something goes wrong, a log is the fastest way to find out why. PCVR Nexus records one only while a stream is running. When no stream is running, nothing is recorded.

What a log contains

  • Phone details: manufacturer and model, Android version, chip, number of CPU cores, memory, screen size and refresh rate.
  • Connection details: Wi‑Fi or USB, Wi‑Fi signal strength, speed and band, and your PC’s name and local address. The name of your Wi‑Fi network is not recorded.
  • Battery and heat: battery level, charging state, temperature and battery-saver state, because heat slows phones down.
  • Your stream settings and the PC’s negotiated settings.
  • Stream health every 5 seconds: frame rate, data rate, dropped frames, delay and audio gaps.
  • Errors and crashes from the app itself, including technical messages from its own process.

How long it is kept

The newest 15 streams are kept; older ones are deleted automatically.

Sharing

A log leaves your phone only if you share it yourself, as a JSON file through Android’s share menu or straight to WhatsApp. It goes to the person and app you choose, and that app’s own privacy policy then applies. The app never uploads logs on its own.

What we never collect

  • Your name, email or phone number
  • Contacts, photos or files
  • Your location
  • Microphone or camera
  • Wi‑Fi network names or MAC addresses
  • Advertising IDs
  • Analytics or usage tracking
  • Recordings of what you stream

Neither app contains advertising, analytics or crash-reporting libraries, and neither app connects to the internet on its own. The only web pages either app opens are this policy and the terms, and only when you tap them.

Network and security

The stream travels directly between your phone and PC over your local Wi‑Fi or a USB cable. It does not cross the internet.

  • A phone can only connect after you type the 4‑digit code shown on the PC.
  • After pairing, the PC checks the phone’s pairing key and accepts motion and control messages only from that phone during a session.
  • You can withdraw trust at any time: Settings › Paired PCs on the phone, or Forget paired phones in PCVR Nexus Host.

To keep the delay as low as possible, the stream is not encrypted. Someone on the same network with the right tools could see it. Use your home network or a USB cable, and avoid streaming on public or shared Wi‑Fi.

Your choices and deleting data

Because everything is on your own devices, you can remove it yourself at any time:

  • Forget a PC on the phone: Settings › Paired PCs.
  • Forget phones on the PC: Forget paired phones in PCVR Nexus Host.
  • Remove everything on the phone: uninstall PCVR Nexus, or clear its storage in Android settings.
  • Remove everything on the PC: uninstall PCVR Nexus Host and delete the folder %LOCALAPPDATA%\GameStreamServer.

Since we hold no data about you, there is nothing for us to export or delete on our side. You are still welcome to contact us with any privacy question.
